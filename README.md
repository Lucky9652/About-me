# About-me
I am passionate about empowering innovation through cloud technology and education.


“Tell me about yourself”

“Hi, I’m Karthik, a DevOps and AWS Cloud Engineer based near Bangalore. I have about a year and a half of hands-on experience building and automating cloud infrastructure and CI/CD pipelines.

Education: I did my Bachelor’s in Mechanical Engineering and then a Master’s in Engineering Design at Anna University. Engineering gave me a strong problem-solving and systems-thinking foundation. During my Master’s I got interested in software and cloud, and I decided to move into that field.

I also completed the C2TC Intern Program with TNS India Foundation and Capgemini. Through these I worked on AWS services like EC2, S3, RDS and IAM, and on tools like Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, Jenkins and Ansible. I also script in Python, Bash and PowerShell, and I set up monitoring with CloudWatch, Prometheus and Grafana. More recently, in August 2026, I completed a Python Using AI workshop to automate more of my work.

Experience: My hands-on project work covers building CI/CD pipelines, containerizing applications with Docker, deploying on Kubernetes, and provisioning infrastructure as code with Terraform. I’m currently working at Suyug Infra, a real estate company, where I build responsive websites and integrate CRM and email lead systems. That gave me real production experience in keeping applications reliable and in working directly with business stakeholders.

Why this role: I’m looking for a DevOps/AWS role where I can apply this skill set on real production systems, automate deployments, improve reliability, and keep growing with a strong engineering team. Your focus on [company’s cloud/automation area] matches what I’ve been building toward, and I’d love to contribute to it.”

3. What is your greatest weakness?

Recommended answer (a real weakness plus what you’re doing about it):

“One area I’ve been working on is that I tend to go deep into a problem before asking for help. When I was learning Kubernetes and Terraform, I would sometimes spend too long troubleshooting on my own, trying to solve everything myself. I realized that in a team environment, especially during production issues, that can cost time. So I now set a time limit. If I’m stuck for about 30 minutes, I check the documentation or community forums and then ask a teammate. This has made me faster and has also improved how I collaborate.”

Alternative (relevant to your background):

“Coming from a mechanical engineering background, I have less experience working on large-scale production systems than someone who has spent years in DevOps roles. I’ve been closing that gap through certifications, hands-on projects and labs, and I’m eager to learn from a team that works on real production workloads.”

Tips:

Choose a weakness that is real but not critical to the job. Avoid saying “I’m a perfectionist” or “I work too hard,” because interviewers see those as dodges.
Always end on what you’re doing to improve.

5.  What is your long-range objective?

Sample answer (about 60-75 seconds):

“Within the next five years, I want to become one of the most reliable DevOps and cloud engineers on your team, someone others turn to when they need to design a solid AWS architecture, fix a pipeline, or automate a manual process. As I build that expertise, I believe I’ll be well prepared to take on bigger responsibilities over the long term, such as leading cloud projects or architecting solutions for the team.

Here is what I’m doing right now to prepare:

Building the technical foundation. I completed an Advanced Certification in Cloud Computing and DevOps with IIT Roorkee and Intellipaat, and I work hands-on with AWS, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, Jenkins and Ansible.
Learning continuously. I recently completed a Python Using AI workshop to automate more of my work, and I’m working toward advanced AWS certifications, such as Solutions Architect or DevOps Engineer Professional.
Practising on real projects. I build and maintain projects on GitHub, including CI/CD pipelines, infrastructure as code and monitoring dashboards with Prometheus and Grafana, so my skills stay practical, not just theoretical.
Staying current. I follow cloud and DevOps trends, AWS announcements and community discussions, because this field changes quickly.
Networking and learning from others. I connect with DevOps professionals on LinkedIn and learn from how experienced engineers solve problems.

My goal is to contribute to your company from day one, keep growing alongside the team, and build a long-term career here.”

Tips:

Keep the objective achievable and tied to the company. “Best engineer on your team” shows commitment. Avoid vague answers like “I want to be a CEO” or answers that suggest you’ll leave soon.
Only claim what’s true. Don’t say you’re pursuing a specific certification unless you really are. If you’re not yet, say “I plan to pursue...” and consider booking the exam, so it becomes true.
Be ready for a follow-up such as “Which certification, and when?” or “Tell me about a project on your GitHub.” Have specific answers.
Match the company. If they use EKS or Terraform heavily, mention that tool as your area of depth.
